Vision, Mission & Network Story

Established 2024 · Kigali, Rwanda · UNICEF-Backed

Our Origin

YAN emerged after a transformative training offered by UNICEF Rwanda — the Youth Advocacy Guide (YAG) — which brought together young leaders across Rwanda. Through that training, participants gained advocacy skills, communication and stakeholder engagement know-how, and a broader network of peers and mentors. Born from the recognition that while individual youth efforts are impactful, a unified front can achieve far greater systemic change.

Vision & Mission

Vision

Building a Rwanda where young people are empowered, actively engaged, and leading transformative advocacy for inclusive and sustainable development.

Mission

To equip and mobilize young advocates with the knowledge, skills, and platforms to champion child rights, social inclusion, and policy change, ensuring meaningful youth participation in national and global development agendas.

Our Philosophy

The Network is anchored on a philosophy that youth have gold in them and the power to decide their life locus and champion change for a better world free from any form of discrimination, harassment, and violence, and that embraces diversity.

Strategic Orientation

  • Strengthening youth voice and agency in societal issues.
  • Promoting solidarity and collaboration among youth‑led organizations.
  • Facilitating capacity building, advocacy, and community‑level impact through collective action.
  • Encouraging sustainable, youth‑driven development and social behavior change across sectors.

Context & Rationale

Rwanda, like many African countries, has a vibrant but fragmented ecosystem of youth‑led organizations (covering health, education, social justice, climate, inclusion, etc.). However, these organizations often face structural limitations: lack of formal capacity in project design and management, inadequate financial and administrative skills, weak governance, and insufficient tools for sustainability.

Nevertheless, given the complexity of issues (nutrition, health, mental health, social behavior, inclusion, rights) and the unpredictability of funding or external support, many youth‑led organizations remain vulnerable, especially in times of resource scarcity, political shifts, or increased demand.
